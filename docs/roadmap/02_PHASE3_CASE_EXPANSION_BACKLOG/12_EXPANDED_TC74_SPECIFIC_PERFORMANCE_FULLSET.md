1. Case Overview (Synthetic)
Case Title: Ramesh Kumar Sharma vs. Suresh Chand Agarwal & Another

Court: Court of the Additional District Judge No. __, Jaipur City

Suit No.: ___ / 2025

Nature: Suit for Specific Performance of Agreement to Sell + Permanent Injunction + Alternative Damages
Parties

Plaintiff: Ramesh Kumar Sharma (buyer)
Defendant No. 1: Suresh Chand Agarwal (seller)
Defendant No. 2: M/s. Apex Builders Pvt. Ltd. (subsequent purchaser – alleged)

Core Transaction

Agreement to Sell dated 15.01.2024 for a residential plot measuring 200 sq. yards in Scheme “Green Valley”, Jaipur.
Total consideration: ₹85,00,000.
Earnest money / part-payment: ₹15,00,000 paid by cheque (cleared).
Balance to be paid on or before 15.07.2024 against simultaneous registration of sale deed.
Time stipulated as essence of the contract.

Plaintiff’s Case

Plaintiff was always ready and willing to perform. He issued multiple notices, got a demand draft prepared for the balance, and remained present on the scheduled date. Defendant No. 1 failed to clear title defects and later executed a sale deed in favour of Defendant No. 2. Plaintiff seeks specific performance, permanent injunction against further alienation, and in the alternative, refund with damages and interest.
Defence Theme

Plaintiff was never ready and willing.
Time was essence and plaintiff committed default.
Title was clear; plaintiff raised frivolous objections.
Subsequent sale to Defendant No. 2 is bona fide and protected.
Suit is barred by limitation / not maintainable for want of continuous readiness.

Key Statutory Provisions

Specific Relief Act, 1963 – Sections 10, 14, 16, 20, 21
Transfer of Property Act, 1882
Code of Civil Procedure – Order 39 Rules 1 & 2, Order 2 Rule 2, Section 34
Limitation Act, 1963 – Article 54
Indian Contract Act, 1872 – Sections 51–55 (reciprocal promises, time essence)

SUIT FOR SPECIFIC PERFORMANCE OF AGREEMENT TO SELL
DATED 15.01.2024, PERMANENT INJUNCTION AND ALTERNATIVE RELIEF OF DAMAGES

MOST RESPECTFULLY SHOWETH:

1. That the Plaintiff is a law-abiding citizen and the Defendants are residents / companies within the territorial jurisdiction of this Hon’ble Court.

2. That on 15.01.2024 the Plaintiff and Defendant No. 1 entered into a written Agreement to Sell in respect of a residential plot measuring 200 square yards situated in Scheme “Green Valley”, Jaipur, more particularly described in Schedule-A hereto (hereinafter “the suit property”).

3. That the total sale consideration was fixed at ₹85,00,000 (Rupees Eighty-Five Lakhs only). The Plaintiff paid a sum of ₹15,00,000 by Cheque No. ______ drawn on ______ Bank, which was duly cleared on 20.01.2024. The balance consideration of ₹70,00,000 was agreed to be paid on or before 15.07.2024 against simultaneous execution and registration of the sale deed.

4. That the Agreement expressly stipulated that time shall be the essence of the contract. However, the conduct of the parties and the surrounding circumstances show that the real intention was that the Plaintiff would be given reasonable opportunity to perform once title was made marketable.

5. That the Plaintiff has always been and continues to be ready and willing to perform his part of the contract. He got a Demand Draft of ₹70,00,000 prepared on 10.07.2024 and remained present at the office of the Sub-Registrar on 15.07.2024 along with the Demand Draft and necessary stamp duty. Defendant No. 1 neither remained present nor executed the sale deed.

6. That the Plaintiff issued Legal Notices dated 05.05.2024 and 20.07.2024 calling upon Defendant No. 1 to perform the Agreement. The notices were duly served.

7. That to the shock and surprise of the Plaintiff, Defendant No. 1, in flagrant breach of the Agreement, executed a registered Sale Deed dated 12.09.2024 in favour of Defendant No. 2 in respect of the very same suit property.

8. That Defendant No. 2 is not a bona fide purchaser without notice. The prior Agreement was known or ought to have been known.

9. That the Plaintiff is entitled to a decree of specific performance directing Defendant No. 1 to execute the sale deed in favour of the Plaintiff upon receipt of the balance consideration, and directing Defendant No. 2 to join in the conveyance or suffer the decree.

10. That the Plaintiff is also entitled to a permanent injunction restraining the Defendants from alienating, transferring, or creating any third-party interest in the suit property.

11. That in the alternative, without prejudice, the Plaintiff claims refund of ₹15,00,000 together with damages, interest and costs.

12. That the cause of action arose on 15.01.2024 when the Agreement was executed, on 15.07.2024 when Defendant No. 1 failed to perform, on 12.09.2024 when the subsequent sale was executed, and is still continuing.

13. That the suit is within limitation. The valuation for the purpose of court fee and jurisdiction is ₹85,00,000. Ad-valorem court fee has been paid.

PRAYER

It is, therefore, most respectfully prayed that this Hon’ble Court may be pleased to:

(a) Pass a decree of specific performance of the Agreement to Sell dated 15.01.2024 directing Defendant No. 1 to execute and register the sale deed in favour of the Plaintiff in respect of the suit property described in Schedule-A upon payment of the balance consideration of ₹70,00,000, and further directing Defendant No. 2 to join in the execution of the sale deed or to suffer the decree;

(b) Pass a decree of permanent injunction restraining the Defendants, their agents, servants and any person claiming through them from alienating, transferring, selling, mortgaging or creating any third-party interest in the suit property;

(c) In the alternative, pass a decree for refund of ₹15,00,000 together with damages, interest at 12% per annum and costs;

(d) Award costs of the suit; and

(e) Pass any other order or orders as this Hon’ble Court may deem fit and proper.
