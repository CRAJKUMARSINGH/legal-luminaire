## Key Legal Standards

### Dying Declaration (Section 32 of Indian Evidence Act)
- Must be recorded by Magistrate whenever possible
- If recorded by non-Magistrate, must be certified as voluntary and fit
- Medical certificate of mental fitness required
- Supreme Court: Panneerselvam vs. State (2020) - oral dying declarations viewed with suspicion

### Chain of Custody (FSL Evidence)
- Continuous documentation of possession from collection to analysis
- Rajasthan FSL Manual: maximum 24-hour gap allowed without explanation
- BNSS Section 53: detailed seizure memo mandatory
- Supreme Court: State vs. Nisha (2022) - broken chain may exclude evidence

### Post-mortem Protocol
- Ministry of Health Guidelines 2019
- Must be conducted by registered medical practitioner
- Detailed injury documentation with photographs
- Time of death estimation based on rigor mortis, liver temperature

### Section 27 Recovery (Disclosure Statement)
- Supreme Court: State vs. Kathikalu (1962) - requirements for admissibility
- Must be made to police officer not below rank of SI
- Information must lead to discovery
- Independent witnesses required for credibility
