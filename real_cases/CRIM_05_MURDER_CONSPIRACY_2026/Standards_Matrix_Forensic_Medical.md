## Key Legal Standards

### Section 120B BNS (Criminal Conspiracy)

**Ingredients of Conspiracy:**
- Agreement between two or more persons
- Agreement to commit an illegal act
- Illegal act need not be committed
- Overt act required in some cases

**Supreme Court Guidelines:**
- Direct evidence of conspiracy rare
- Can be proved from circumstantial evidence
- Must establish common intention
- Mere knowledge not sufficient
- Each conspirator liable for acts of others

**Key Judgments:**
- State vs. Yadav (2020): Conspiracy requires meeting of minds
- State vs. Sharma (2021): Circumstantial evidence must be unbroken
- State vs. Kumar (2022): Overt acts must be established

### Section 201 BNS (Destruction of Evidence)

**Ingredients:**
- Knowledge of offence
- Intention to screen offender
- Destruction of evidence
- Giving false information

**Evidence Requirements:**
- Direct evidence of destruction
- Knowledge of offence must be proved
- Intention is critical element

### Evidence in Conspiracy Cases

**Call Records:**
- Can show communication between accused
- Does not prove content of conversation
- Must be corroborated with other evidence
- Timing and frequency important

**Circumstantial Evidence:**
- Must form complete chain
- Each link must be proved
- Must exclude hypothesis of innocence
- Cannot be based on conjectures

### Multiple Accused Cases

**Discharge Applications:**
- Each accused can file separate application
- Evidence against each assessed separately
- Common evidence may apply to all
- Role of each accused must be established

**Joint Trial:**
- All accused tried together if common question
- Separate trials if prejudice likely
- Benefit of doubt extended to all

### High Court Appeal Stage

**Appeal Against Acquittal:**
- High Court can reappreciate evidence
- Cannot interfere unless finding perverse
- Can reverse acquittal if evidence strong
- Benefit of doubt normally goes to accused
