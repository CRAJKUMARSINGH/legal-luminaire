# 00 — Start Here

An eleven-week supervised curriculum for Rajasthan district and High Court
practice, built on `tutorial-data/pairs`.

## What this course is

A ladder. Week 1 teaches you to say "not yet" to a client. Week 8 teaches you to
take over someone else's half-built file without losing a date. In between you
will learn where forum lives, how a statutory clock runs, what a court fee
actually costs when you get it wrong, and why an appeal dies of a vakalatnama
before it dies of a ground.

Weeks 9–11 are separate gates: the revenue ladder, POCSO bail, and the POCSO
trial. None is required for the certificate. All three are required before you
take the corresponding work unsupervised.

## What this course is not

Not a lecture series. Not a reading list. There is no video, no slide deck, no
"AI will draft it for you" comfort. You write first, badly, and then you compare.

## The rule that makes it work

**You do not open `expected` until you have written your own answer.**

Every pair file has an `expected` block. If you read it before writing, you have
learned to read a table. If you write first, you learn where your own instinct
fails — which is the only thing that transfers to a live counter.

Set up a scratch file per session. Paste only the `input` block. Write. Then
open `expected`.

## Who this is for

- **Beginner track** (weeks 1–2): 0–2 years, mostly intake and drafting support.
- **Intermediate track** (weeks 3–5, 10): 2–6 years, running own files.
- **Advanced track** (weeks 6–7, 9): 6–12 years, contested matters and appeals.
- **Expert track** (weeks 8, 11): 12+, or anyone taking over files mid-stream.

You may enter at your level. You may not skip week 1. Everyone does week 1.

## Time

Four sessions per week, 45–90 minutes each. Sessions 1 and 2 on separate days.
Roughly 55 hours of supervised work across eleven weeks.

## What you need

- The repo running locally, frontend only (`pnpm --filter @workspace/legal-luminaire run dev`)
- A scratch file per session
- A supervisor or study partner for the marking sessions
- The bare Acts listed in `annex/B-section-mapping.md`, open in a second window

## Honesty rules

1. If you look at `expected` early, say so in your log. Do not pretend.
2. If you do not know a section number, write `PENDING` and move on. That is
   what the engine does and what a real filing counter rewards.
3. If your answer is better than the engine's, write it down in
   `seniorVariants` and open a PR. That is a contribution, not a correction.
4. Never write `filing-ready` on anything.
