## Why the order

Triage before forum, because you cannot choose a court for a file you have not
read. Forum before requisition, because what you requisition depends on which
court's rules you are filing under. Requisition before clocks, because a clock
you meet with an empty file is a clock you meet badly.

Clocks and bail sit together at weeks 4–5 because both are time-critical and
both punish hesitation. Pleadings at week 6 is the first week where the output
is a document rather than a decision — by then you have earned it.

Week 8 is the only week where the file arrives already broken by someone else.

## Progression ladder

```
W1–2  Beginner      You decide whether and where. Output: memo, not draft.
W3–5  Intermediate  You build the file. Output: requisition, reply, bail set.
W6–7  Advanced      You commit to a document. Output: pleading, appeal memo.
W8    Expert        You inherit. Output: handover audit and a defended position.
```

Mid-journey appears in week 1 on purpose: even a beginner meets a client who
has already been failed by someone. It is not an advanced-only experience.

## What each week contains

- **Why this week exists** — the failure it is built to catch
- **Objectives** — three, testable
- **Reading** — bare Act sections only, no commentary
- **Session 1** — cold attempt, expected block sealed
- **Session 2** — gate prediction, before you see the real gates
- **Session 3** — comparison and marking against the Rubric
- **Session 4** — rewrite at the next level up
- **Failure modes** — the specific mistakes this week is designed to surface
- **Deliverable and rubric band**
- **Carry-forward** — what week N+1 assumes you took from week N

## Exit standard

You pass the core course (weeks 1–8) when, on a pair you have never seen, you
can predict the `citationGate`, the `limitationGate` and the
`terminalOutcome.kind` correctly, and your own draft is within one rubric band
of the engine's.

Weeks 9–11 are separate gates. None is required for the certificate. All three
are required before taking the corresponding work unsupervised — a revenue
appeal, a POCSO bail, a POCSO trial.

That is a high bar. Most people finish week 8 in band 3 of 5. Band 4 is a good
advocate. Band 5 is a supervisor.
