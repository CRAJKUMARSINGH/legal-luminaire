# Annex H — Session 3 Scripts, Weeks 9–11

Thirty minutes each. Keep to it.

These are scripts, not notes. Where words are in quotes, say them close to as
written. Where they are not, use your own.

Session 3 does not set the band. It generates the observations the band is set
from afterwards. Write your observations during the session, in the margin of
the trainee's own paper.

**Before you start, every week:** put the trainee's draft on the table between
you, face up. Annex D closed until the reveal block. Annex F open, folded so
only the current week shows.

## Week 9 — Script

Run after the split day, not after the morning half.

### Open (2 min)

"The four files are on the table. Yours are the three you wrote. We are going to
go through them in the order you would least like to."

### Reveal 1 — TP-RJ-14, the copy (6 min)

Lay the stamped order down. Do not open Annex D yet.

"Read me the date on the order."

Wait. They will read it.

"Now: how much of the appeal period is left?"

**If they do not know — the usual answer — say nothing for ten seconds.** Let
them sit in it.

"Nobody can tell you, because nobody applied. The period is running and you
have no idea where it stands. Show me the copy application in your file."

There will not be one. Open Annex D, read the limitation gate aloud.

**Counter-move:** "The certified copy application is not paperwork. It is the
first page of the appeal. Until it is filed you are drafting blind and the clock
is drafting against you."

**If they did ask for the copy:** ask what date they applied and where the
receipt is. If there is a receipt, they have earned the band and you say so.

### Reveal 2 — TP-RJ-16, the recording (7 min)

"The SDO recorded your client's statement. Read the paragraph."

They will find it. Give them the time.

"What ground did you write about it?"

**If nothing:** "The strongest ground in this file is a sentence the SDO wrote
against himself, and it is on page two. Your grounds do not mention it. Why
would the Collector think it matters if you do not?"

**If they wrote it, but late in the grounds:** "Move it. What the officer
recorded and then ignored is ground one. It is not a sub-clause."

Then the bribe branch, whichever way it went: "You wrote that the reader demanded
money. What date, what amount?" If both are absent — "An allegation with no date
and no figure is a paragraph the Collector will read as colour. Keep it, but
keep it third."

### Reveal 3 — TP-RJ-24, the win (6 min)

"Read me your reply."

**The usual answer is one line, or none.** Let them read the thin thing aloud.
It will sound thinner out loud than it looked.

"You won at the Collector and you have written a reply the Commissioner could
dismiss without opening the file. A win is not a decision. Which rung is the
Commissioner?"

Wait for the rung. Then: "So how much of this ladder is left?"

**Counter-move:** "The client will tell you it is over. The client is not wrong
to think so, and is wrong. Your job this week was to be the one person in the
room who knew it."

### Reveal 4 — TP-RJ-13, the board (5 min)

If TP-RJ-13 ran, go to the board and correct it rung by rung, in front of them.
Start from the bottom, not the top.

If TP-RJ-13 was skipped, draw the four rungs yourself and make them copy it.

At the fourth rung: "This one is unverified in your own material. Nobody in this
room knows the provision. Say so when you teach it."

### Close (2 min)

Have them write, verbatim:

**"A revenue file is lost by the gap you left, not by the argument you lost."**

### Observations to record

Whether the copy was applied for; whether the recording was found and where it
sat in the grounds; whether a reply existed at all; whether the fourth rung was
drawn unprompted.

## Week 10 — Script

Run in the same sitting as the prompts. Do not let a day pass.

### Open (2 min)

Put both drafts down side by side.

"Two files, three days apart, same papers. You will read me both, and then you
will tell me which paragraphs changed."

### Reveal 1 — TP-RJ-15, the wrong frame (7 min)

Take the first draft.

"What section is the FIR under?"

**If they know:** "Good. Now show me where the draft uses it." There will be
nothing there.

**If they do not know:** "You opposed bail without reading what he is charged
with. Read it now."

Then: "Did you write a limitation period anywhere?" When they did — "There is
none in POCSO. You have put a clock on a file that has no clock and you have
spent the urgency on it."

Then the third paragraph: "Whose words are these?" If it is the parent's account
written as the child's — "You have put the mother in the witness box and called
her the child. When she is cross-examined on it, the answer will be hers and the
affidavit will be wrong."

### Reveal 2 — TP-RJ-17, the inversion (8 min)

Take the second draft.

"Different family, same child, three days later. What is your ground one?"

**The usual answer is gravity.** "Serious offence. He will abscond." Say: "The
bench has already weighed gravity. It granted bail. What did it not weigh?"

If the messages appear: "Quote me one." They will have to open the chronology.
"Now put the message in the draft, dated. 'He has been contacting her' is what
the client says. One message is what the court reads."

Then the prayer: "You have prayed for refusal. And then?" If no conditions —
"You have asked for a door to be shut and not said what holds it shut. Name
three conditions. Now."

### Reveal 3 — the joint block (8 min)

Back to both drafts, side by side.

"Mark every paragraph where the posture changed. Then mark every paragraph where
it should have changed and did not."

This is the payload of the week. Give it the time. Read the second set of marks
aloud.

Then ask, once: "What did you assume about the child that the papers do not
say?"

### Close (2 min)

**"Opposing bail and resisting bail are different documents. Same papers, same
child, different sentences."**

### Observations to record

Whether the sections were read; whether a limitation period was invented;
whether parent and child were separated; whether a message was quoted verbatim;
how many conditions were named without prompting; how many posture marks landed
in the second set.

## Week 11 — Script

Run at the end of the sitting, with all three waves' papers in hand.

### Open (2 min)

Lay the three papers out in wave order.

"Three versions of one outline. We will read the last one first, then work
backwards to why it changed."

### Reveal 1 — wave 3, the discrepancy (5 min)

Read the wave 3 treatment aloud.

"Where does this sit now — chief or cross?"

**If chief:** "Then you have decided to meet it. Say the sentence you would put
to her." Make them say it, out loud, in the room, as the child.

**If cross:** "You have left it for the defence to find. What do they do with a
date she got wrong?"

Then read the wave 1 treatment of the same fact. The comparison is the reveal.
Ask nothing else.

### Reveal 2 — wave 1, the leading questions (8 min)

Read the outline aloud, you as the child. **Answer every leading question with
one word and stop.** "Yes." "No." "Correct." Let it get dull. It will get dull
fast.

Stop at the third or fourth. "How many so far?"

Count them out loud from the page. All of them, not just the first four.

"Every one of those is a sentence you said and she agreed to. How much of her
account is in this document?"

Then the clock: "Time it." Read it again at pace. It will overrun.

### Reveal 3 — wave 2, the room (7 min)

"Where does the mother sit?"

**The usual answer is behind or beside the child.** "Then the child answers the
advocate's questions looking at her mother's face. When the answer is wrong, she
will see it on the face before she finishes the sentence. Move the mother. Where?"

Then: "Show me the screen application." If there is none — "You left it to the
court. The court will not ask. What does s. 33(5) require of you before she
walks in?"

### Reveal 4 — the read-through (6 min)

Take wave 3. Read it once more, at pace, with the corrections in place.

Time it. If it is inside twenty minutes, say so and say what made the
difference.

### Close (2 min)

**"Every word she says is a word she has to carry. Say fewer of them."**

### Observations to record

Where the discrepancy sat in each wave and whether it moved; leading-question
count in wave 1 and in wave 3; whether the screen was applied for in writing;
where the mother was seated; read-aloud time against the twenty-minute ceiling.

## Running these badly

The three ways session 3 goes wrong, all of them the facilitator's:

- **Reading Annex D as a list.** It is an answer key, not a script. The reveal is
  the trainee's own sentence coming back at them.
- **Filling the silence.** The ten seconds after "how much of the period is
  left?" is the most instructive moment of week 9. Do not rescue it.
- **Letting a correct answer skip the reveal.** If they got it right, the reveal
  is *how* they got it right. Ask for the reasoning, then move on — do not
  congratulate and jump.
