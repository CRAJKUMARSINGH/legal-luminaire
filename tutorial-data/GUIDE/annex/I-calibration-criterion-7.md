# Annex I — Calibration, Criterion 7

One fact pattern, two outlines, marked. Read the notes in the margin of the
first. Do not read the second until you have banded the first on your own.

Facts, fixed for both: child, 14, tuition student. Accused is the tuition
teacher. Her 183 statement says the incident happened "after the Diwali test".
The school diary places the Diwali test a week earlier than she says. A WhatsApp
message she saved is dated the day after the date she gives.

Section numbering for examination is PENDING in this fixture. Mark the craft,
not the sections.

## Document A

> Q1. You are fourteen years old, correct?
> Q2. The accused was your tuition teacher, correct?
> Q3. He used to teach you at his house, correct?
> Q4. On 12 November, after the Diwali test, you had gone for tuition, correct?
> Q5. On that day he took you to the inner room, correct?
> Q6. He closed the door, correct?
> Q7. He then caught hold of your hand, correct?
> Q8. He then committed the assault, correct?
> Q9. You told your mother that evening, correct?
> Q10. Your mother asked you what happened, correct?
> Q11. You told her the same thing you have told this court, correct?
> Q12. A message was sent to you the next day, correct?
> Q13. That message is on the phone, correct?
> Q14. The phone is yours, correct?
> Q15. You saved that message, correct?
> Q16. You have brought the phone today, correct?
> Q17. That is all. Please show the court the message.

**Notes.**

**Q1–Q3, the leading run.** Three closed questions to establish a fourteen-year-
old's age and the tuition relationship. Every answer is "yes". Nothing is wrong
with any single line; the problem is that by Q4 she has said nothing that is
hers. Band 2 on 7 begins here and it is a pattern, not a question.

**Q4, the date.** "On 12 November" — the advocate supplies the date and she
agrees. This is the discrepancy already being buried: if she agrees to the
advocate's date she never has to reconcile it with the diary, and the conflict
goes into the record unresolved. Note the wording, "after the Diwali test",
which is the 183 phrase, lifted rather than asked about.

**Q5–Q8, the incident in four closed steps.** "He took you", "he closed", "he
caught", "he committed". Four sentences that are the advocate's account of the
assault. She has confirmed it. She has not described it. On a document like this
the cross-examiner's first question is "which of these did you actually see?" and
the child has no description of her own to fall back on.

**Q9–Q11, the mother substituted.** Three questions about what she told her
mother, and the answers are the mother's account wearing the child's voice. Q11
("the same thing you have told this court") is also circular — it assumes she has
told this court anything yet, which on this document she has not.

**Q12–Q16, the message in five closed steps.** The same fault as the incident,
applied to the one piece of documentary evidence in the file. Five yeses and the
message is proved in the abstract. Nowhere is its text put to her, so the words
in it are never in her mouth.

**Q17, the close.** "That is all" and a request to show the phone. No
identification of the sender in her words, no date taken from her, no fallback
if the phone is objected to.

**Length.** Read at pace this runs about 35 minutes. Roughly double the ceiling,
and the overrun is caused by the volume of questions, not by their difficulty.

**Accommodation.** Absent from the document. On the day: no screen application
in the file, mother seated directly behind the child, so every answer is
delivered into her mother's face. The child corrects herself twice mid-answer
and both corrections are audible.

**Band.** Criterion 7: **band 2**. The examination is orderly and it will produce
a record. It is the advocate's account from the first question to the last, and
the child is a confirming instrument in it.

## Document B

> Q1. What is your name?
> Q2. How old are you?
> Q3. Which class?
> Q4. Who used to teach you tuition?
> Q5. Where did the tuition happen?
> Q6. Who else was in that house when you went?
> Q7. Tell the court what happened on the day you remember.
> Q8. You have said "after the Diwali test". When was the test?
> Q9. And the incident was after that?
> Q10. Was it the same day or a later day?
> Q11. How many days later, as best you remember?
> Q12. You saved a message on your phone. When did it come — before or after the
>      incident?
> Q13. Read the message to the court.
> Q14. Who sent it?
> Q15. How do you know it was him?
> Q16. You told your mother. What did you tell her?
> Q17. Is there anything you want to add?

**Notes.**

**Q1–Q3, identity in open form.** Three questions, three answers that are hers,
twenty seconds. Age arrives as her answer rather than as a proposition she
agrees with.

**Q4–Q6, relationship and place, still open.** Q6 is doing quiet work — "who else
was in that house" fixes the setting without the advocate describing it, and
gives the cross-examiner less to say about an empty house.

**Q7, the incident in one open question.** The single most important line in the
document. Whatever she says here is hers and survives cross because it cannot be
attributed to the advocate. If she omits something, the advocate has the rest of
the chief to fill it, and the fill is visibly a fill.

**Q8–Q11, the discrepancy met in chief.** Four questions that walk her to her own
date without correcting it. Q10 offers "same day or later" rather than a date, so
she is not agreeing with the advocate. Q11 accepts an approximation, which is
what a fourteen-year-old actually has. The conflict is now on the record as her
account, and the defence has to dislodge it rather than discover it.

**Q12, the message anchored by time.** Before or after — a binary, but a binary
about a fact she holds, and it places the message relative to the incident in her
words.

**Q13–Q15, the message read, not described.** Q13 puts the words in her mouth as
the sender wrote them. Q15 ("how do you know it was him") is the identification
the defence would otherwise make her supply under cross, extracted here on her
own terms.

**Q16, the mother's account, still open.** "What did you tell her" rather than
"you told her the same thing" — so the mother's version is hers to give, and any
gap between the two is hers to explain first.

**Q17, the close.** One open door. No "that is all" abruptness, and room for her
to add the thing she has been holding.

**Length.** About 16 minutes at pace. Inside the ceiling with margin.

**Accommodation.** Screen applied for in writing two days before, on the file.
Mother seated at the child's front left, in her eyeline but not behind her, so
the child can look at her or not. The child corrects herself once and it is not
audible as a correction.

**Band.** Criterion 7: **band 4**. Her account, extracted with discipline, with
the known weakness met before the defence reached it.

## What band 5 adds

Not more questions. Three things, and they are all preparation rather than
examination:

- **The chief is built around what she can actually do**, not around the elements
  of the offence. If she cannot read fluently, Q13 becomes "tell the court what
  the message says" and the phone is shown instead of read.
- **A fallback for the message** if it is objected to — she describes receiving
  it and the certificate route is held in reserve.
- **The accommodation settled with the child, not for her** — she is asked
  whether she wants the screen before it is applied for, and the answer is on the
  file.

Band 5 is rare in a cohort. One in a group of twelve, at most.

## Calibration rules

- **Mark criterion 7 alone.** Do not let a strong triage or a clean draft pull
  the number up. Document A above would score band 3 or 4 on triage and band 2
  on 7, and both are correct.
- **Count leading questions before you read for anything else.** The count, not
  the impression, is the first fact.
- **Time both documents yourself.** Do not take the trainee's word for the
  length.
- **The accommodation is marked even when the outline is good.** An excellent
  chief with the mother behind the child is not band 4.
- **If you banded A at 3, re-read Q7 and Q13** and ask who is speaking in each.
