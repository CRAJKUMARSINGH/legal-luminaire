# Week 3 — Document Requisition

**Level** intermediate · **Pairs** TP-RJ-01, TP-RJ-07 · **Time** 4 × 60 min
**Prerequisite** weeks 1–2.

## Why this week exists

Every file contains one sentence that decides it, and it is almost never in the
client's first account. Requisition is the skill of going and finding that
sentence. This week also teaches the harder half: requisitioning from an
uncooperative source — a previous advocate, a department, a bank.

## Objectives

1. Build a requisition list that is specific, obtainable and forum-appropriate.
2. Separate documents the *client* can get from documents that need an
   application, a fee, or a certified-copy request.
3. Requisition evidence that hurts, not only evidence that helps.

## Reading

CPC O. 11 (discovery) and O. 13 (production), and the Rajasthan High Court
Rules on certified copies (Chapter on Copying, verify section). Annex C entries
for jamabandi, girdawari, naksha, khasra.

## Session 1 — Cold attempt (60 min)

TP-RJ-01: write a requisition list. Aim for eight items. Mark each with how it
is obtained — client, certified copy, application, or third-party request — and
how long it takes.

Then TP-RJ-07: same, focused on what a new advocate must recover from a
non-responsive predecessor.

## Session 2 — The hostile requisition (45 min)

For TP-RJ-07, write the letter to the previous advocate. Then write the list of
what the client can obtain *without* that advocate's cooperation, because that
list is what keeps the file moving.

## Session 3 — Reveal and mark (60 min)

Open both expected blocks. Compare lists item by item. The engine's TP-RJ-01
list includes girdawari and naksha — possession and location evidence. Did
yours? The engine's TP-RJ-07 list includes the client's own follow-up record.
Did you think to requisition the client's memory?

## Session 4 — Rewrite (60 min)

Rebuild the TP-RJ-07 requisition list at band 4 with three columns: obtainable
by client, obtainable by application, obtainable only from the advocate. Then
write the two-line paragraph that tells the client which column to start on.

## Failure modes this week catches

- **Helping-only requisition.** Aadhaar, deed, photographs — and nothing that
  could cut against the client.
- **Fantasy requisition.** Asking for documents that do not exist or cannot be
  compelled.
- **Advocate-dependency.** A list that cannot progress until a hostile person
  cooperates.
- **Uncosted requisition.** No sense of which item takes a day and which takes
  three weeks.

## Deliverable

The three-column TP-RJ-07 list and the client instruction paragraph.

## Carry-forward

Week 4 puts you on a clock. You will meet that clock with whatever your
requisition produced. Trainees who requisitioned broadly in week 3 meet week 4
calmly; the rest meet it with an empty file.
