# Week 4 — Statutory Clocks and Replies

**Level** intermediate · **Pairs** TP-RJ-02, TP-RJ-04, TP-RJ-09 · **Time** 4 × 75 min
**Prerequisite** weeks 1–3.

## Why this week exists

A statutory clock cannot be stopped, only met. This week covers the three
commonest clocks in district practice — a s. 138 reply window, a notice reply
with an admitted liability, and a High Court show-cause — and teaches the
discipline of anchoring every deadline to the correct date.

## Objectives

1. Identify the operative anchor date for a clock, from the document, not from
   the client's recollection.
2. Draft a reply that preserves the defence without making a false admission.
3. Recognise when a reply must go out today regardless of how incomplete it is.

## Reading

NI Act ss. 138 (with provisos), 139, 145. BNSS s. 144. Constitution Art. 226 on
laches. Annex B rows for 138 and 226.

## Session 1 — Cold attempt (75 min)

TP-RJ-02: write the reply. Anchor the date first, on the page, from the RPAD
cover — not from the notice date.

TP-RJ-04: the client has instructed three points, one of which ("notice is bad
for wrong address") is only half right. Write the reply the client asked for,
then write the version you would actually send.

## Session 2 — Anchor and clock (45 min)

For all three pairs, write: the anchor document, the anchor date, the deadline,
and the days remaining. For TP-RJ-09 note that the clock is a court-ordered
date, not a statutory one — and say why that changes the pressure.

## Session 3 — Reveal and mark (solo)

Working alone? Do not open `expected` yet. Set the artifact aside for four
hours minimum, then run the week's presence and absence checklists in
Annex E. Score literally. Write the three-sentence self-challenge before you
open `expected`.

With a partner, mark in pairs against the Rubric and compare bands before
discussing.

## Session 4 — Rewrite (75 min)

Rewrite TP-RJ-09 at band 4. Requirements:
- concede the dates openly
- reframe the grievance to the unpaid co-sharer share
- address the reference objection instead of ignoring it
- flag the affidavit conflict rather than defending it

## Failure modes this week catches

- **Wrong anchor.** Notice date instead of receipt date. Costs the client the
  reply right outright.
- **Client-instruction literalism.** Drafting the half-true point because the
  client said it.
- **Defensive affidavit.** Defending an over-claimed date instead of correcting
  it.
- **Waiting for completeness.** Holding a reply that had to go out today.

## Deliverable

The TP-RJ-09 rewrite and a clock sheet for all three pairs showing anchor,
deadline and days remaining.

## Carry-forward

Week 5 moves from documents to custody. The clock discipline carries directly:
bail applications are lost by people who anchored to the wrong date.
