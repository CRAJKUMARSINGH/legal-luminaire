# Week 5 — FIR, Arrest and Bail

**Level** intermediate · **Pairs** TP-RJ-03, TP-RJ-06 · **Time** 4 × 75 min
**Prerequisite** weeks 1–4.

## Why this week exists

Beginners argue the section. The section is not the emergency. This week drills
the correct order of operations when custody is in play: protect, record,
then argue.

## Objectives

1. Separate protective relief (bail, appearance) from strategic relief
   (quashing, discharge) and sequence them correctly.
2. Read an FIR for what it pleads badly, not only for what it pleads.
3. Identify the s. 35(3) BNSS question, which changes every arrest calculus.

## Reading

BNSS ss. 35, 35(3), 316(2), 318(4), 440, 482, 528. Annex B rows for 316, 318,
440, 482, 528. Read s. 35(3) slowly — it is short and it is the whole week.

## Session 1 — Cold attempt (75 min)

TP-RJ-03: the client is called for interrogation on Monday. Write the
interrogation brief — what he takes, what he says, what he does not say.

Then write the one-paragraph note to the IO placing the documents on the
investigation file.

## Session 2 — Sequence (45 min)

List, in order, every step from today to the first hearing, for both TP-RJ-03
and TP-RJ-06. For TP-RJ-06 note that the vakalatnama is signed by the brother
and the certified copies do not exist — both are defects ahead of any ground.

## Session 3 — Reveal and mark (solo)

Working alone? Do not open `expected` yet. Set the artifact aside for four
hours minimum, then run the week's presence and absence checklists in
Annex E. Score literally. Write the three-sentence self-challenge before you
open `expected`.

With a partner, mark in pairs against the Rubric and compare bands before
discussing.

## Session 4 — Rewrite (75 min)

Rewrite the TP-RJ-06 sequence at band 4. It must start with certified copies
and the jail vakalatnama, and must put the s. 440 application ahead of the
appeal. Grounds come last.

## Failure modes this week catches

- **Quash reflex.** Filing s. 528 within days of an FIR because the client wants
  it "cancelled".
- **Merits in bail.** Arguing the defence in a suspension application instead of
  custody, parity and sentence.
- **Relative's vakalatnama.** Filing on a signature that is not the appellant's.
- **Order inversion.** Grounds before record, appeal before suspension.

## Deliverable

The rewritten TP-RJ-06 sequence plus a short note on why protective relief
precedes strategic relief.

## Carry-forward

Week 6 is the first week you commit to a document. Everything you sequenced
here becomes the skeleton of a pleading.
