# Week 6 — Pleadings, Valuation and Court Fee

**Level** advanced · **Pairs** TP-RJ-05, TP-RJ-11 · **Time** 4 × 75 min
**Prerequisite** weeks 1–5.

## Why this week exists

An advanced file fails on the boring item. In TP-RJ-05 the bundle is complete,
the prima facie case is strong, and the plaint still cannot be instituted until
the court fee is right. This week teaches you to hunt the boring item before it
hunts you.

## Objectives

1. Value a suit on the correct basis and compute the fee from the Act, not from
   the deed.
2. Plead a bar and its exception in the plaint itself, pre-emptively.
3. Identify the clause that looks like the other side's defence and read it as
   your own limitation grant (TP-RJ-11).

## Reading

Specific Relief Act ss. 34, 38. CPC O. 7 rr. 1–11, O. 39 rr. 1–2. Rajasthan
Court Fees and Suits Valuation Act 1961 — find the declaration and injunction
sections yourself. RERA Act s. 14(3), s. 31. Annex B.

## Session 1 — Cold attempt (75 min)

TP-RJ-05: write the plaint's valuation and court-fee paragraph, and the O. 39
application's prima facie paragraph. Then list what the municipal set-back
notice does to your case.

TP-RJ-11: read the agreement's defect clause. Write whether it helps or hurts,
and the notice paragraph that uses it.

## Session 2 — Fee computation (45 min)

Compute the fee on TP-RJ-05 three ways: deed consideration, circle rate, and
market value. State which the Act requires and what changes if you are wrong.
Then find the section number and mark it `SECONDARY` if unverified.

## Session 3 — Reveal and mark (solo)

Working alone? Do not open `expected` yet. Set the artifact aside for four
hours minimum, then run the week's presence and absence checklists in
Annex E. Score literally. Write the three-sentence self-challenge before you
open `expected`.

With a partner, mark in pairs against the Rubric and compare bands before
discussing.

## Session 4 — Rewrite (75 min)

Rewrite the TP-RJ-11 notice opening at band 4: possession date, defect,
engineer's causation finding, rectification demand, consequential loss,
compliance period — as a statutory s. 14(3) notice, not a complaint letter.

## Failure modes this week catches

- **Deed-value valuation.** Using the 1998 consideration because it is written
  in the deed.
- **Unpled bar.** A plaint with no paragraph on s. 207.
- **Clause misread.** Reading the five-year defect clause as the developer's
  shield.
- **Ignored landmine.** Discovering the municipal notice in the reply.

## Deliverable

The fee computation sheet with the Act's section identified, and the TP-RJ-11
notice opening.

## Carry-forward

Week 7 takes a decided matter up. Valuation, fee and pleading discipline all
reappear in the appeal memo and the delay application.
